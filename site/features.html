Syntax Highlighting

Kode highlights more than 300 languages with KDE's KSyntaxHighlighting, the engine of Kate and Qt Creator.

  • Detected by file name, extension and shebang; the status bar shows the language and changes it from a menu of every definition by section.
  • Themes in pairs: Kode (Qt Creator's 2024 colors), Atom One, ayu, Breeze, GitHub, gruvbox and Solarized, each with a light and a dark version that follows the app's appearance.
  • Brackets and words: the bracket matching the one at the caret, and the word at the caret with its occurrences in view, are marked behind the text.
  • Indent guides show each level, the block at the caret in the accent color.
  • Semantic colors from the language server: types, fields, locals, parameters and macros in C and C++, QML's types, ids and bindings, in Qt Creator's palette.
  • A minimap beside the text (View ▸ Show Minimap): click a line to center it, drag to scroll.

Language Servers

Kode talks to the language server of each file it opens (LSP), found on your shell's PATH: clangd for C, C++ and Objective-C, and servers for Python, Rust, Go, JavaScript and TypeScript, QML, Swift, Bash, Lua, Java, Kotlin, YAML, JSON, HTML, CSS, Markdown, CMake, Dockerfile, Terraform and TOML.

  • Problems as you type: a wave under each error or warning, its message where the pointer rests, and the counts in the status bar; a click there lists them and goes to one.
  • Quick fixes: ⌥↩ shows the server's fixes and refactorings at the caret, as Qt Creator's Alt+Return; one undo step.
  • Completion as a word starts, after . or ->, or on ⌃Space; keep typing to filter, ↩ or Tab to accept. Includes the server adds come in the same undo step.
  • What the server is doing (starting, analyzing, indexing) shows in the sidebar's footer.
  • Settings ▸ Language Servers: turn them off, add folders to look in, set clangd's threads, add your own server for any language, and see what was found — press and hold a missing one to copy its install command.

Navigation

Getting around a project without the mouse, and back again.

  • Go to Definition: F2 or ⌘-click; holding ⌘ over a name shows it as a link. Several definitions open a menu. In the sidebar, F2 still renames.
  • Switch Header/Source: F4, as in Qt Creator — the file beside it, else the one clangd knows.
  • Back and Forward through places, not only files: each split remembers where you edited, clicked on another line or jumped from, as Qt Creator does (⌃⌘← ⌃⌘→, or the split's arrows).
  • Find File: ⌘K, or the magnifying glass in the sidebar, as Qt Creator's Locate: the folder's files whose name matches, the best first — names starting with what you typed, then holding it, then its letters at word starts — each with its path.
  • Go to Line: ⌘L, "line" or "line:column".

Editing

The keys you expect from a Mac text view, and the ones you expect from a code editor.

  • Indentation: tabs or spaces and the width, detected when a file opens and changed from the status bar. Tab and ⇧Tab or ⌘] ⌘[ shift the selected lines.
  • Brackets and quotes in pairs: typing an opening one adds its closing one, typing the closing one steps over it, and ⌫ in an empty pair takes both. ↩ after a { adds the } two lines down.
  • Auto-indent by the brackets, without knowing the language: ↩ between brackets puts the closing one on a line of its own, and a closing bracket lines up with its opening line. ⌃I re-indents the selection, and Kode can re-indent on save.
  • Comments by the language's own markers: ⌘/ toggles line comments, ⌥⌘/ a block comment.
  • Select Block: ⌘U selects the block around the selection, a larger one each time; ⇧⌘U steps back.
  • Wrap lines and show invisibles from the View menu; drag and drop text, ⌥ to copy.
  • Input methods, press-and-hold accents and VoiceOver work in the editor.

Multiple Carets

Edit several places at once, as in VS Code and Qt Creator.

  • ⌃D selects the word at the caret, then adds the next occurrence of the selection with a caret of its own (whole words when it started from a caret).
  • ⌥↑ ⌥↓ add a caret on the line above or below at the same column; ⌥⇧↑ ⌥⇧↓ keep the selection's columns, a block selection.
  • Typing, deleting, pasting, ← → with ⌥, ⌘ or ⇧, and Home/End act at every caret; the edit is one undo step. Any other move or esc leaves one caret.

Code Folding

Hide the blocks you are not reading.

  • ⌥⌘← / ⌥⌘→ fold and unfold the block at the caret, as in Xcode; View ▸ Code Folding also unfolds everything.
  • Markers in the gutter (▾ / ▸) toggle a block; a ⋯ after a folded line opens it.
  • Blocks come from the language: KSyntaxHighlighting's folding regions, or indentation where the language works that way (Python, YAML).
  • The caret never hides: going to a line, a match or an undo inside a fold opens it.

Find & Replace

⌘F opens the find bar under the split's title bar, ⌘R with replace.

  • ⌘G / ⇧⌘G for the next and previous match, ⌘E to search for the selection.
  • Match case and regular expressions, with $0…$9 in the replacement.
  • Every match in view is marked, with the count; the search runs in slices, so even a 100 MB file never stalls the window.
  • Replace All is a single undo step.
  • Go to line with ⌘L: 42 or 42:7.

Splits

Every window is a tree of splits, as in Qt Creator, and every split shows one of the app's open documents.

  • ⌘D / ⇧⌘D split side by side / top and bottom, ⌥⌘D removes a split. Splits nest as deep as you like.
  • The same document in two splits, each with its own caret and scroll, edits showing in both.
  • Back and forward (⌃⌘← / ⌃⌘→) through the documents a split showed, like a browser.
  • ⌃Tab switches among the open documents, the split's most recent first; the bar's pop-up lists them all.

Sidebar

Open a folder and the window shows it in a sidebar, as Xcode's navigator and VS Code's explorer (⌃⌘S hides it).

  • The Finder's order and icons: folders first, names sorted numerically, code files badged with their language.
  • Synchronize with Editor reveals the file in view; Enclosing Folder and Make Root move the window's folder.
  • File operations from the right-click menu: New File, New Folder, Rename (named in place), Duplicate, Move to Trash, Cut, Copy and Paste — with the Finder too — Copy Path and Reveal in Finder.
  • Open documents follow the files you rename or move.

Documents

Kode works with the Mac's document model, not against it.

  • Open from anywhere: the Finder's Open With and Open in Kode (right-click a folder or a file), the Dock, File ▸ Open Recent. A file already open is shown where it is.
  • The kode command, as VS Code's code: install it from Settings ▸ Files, then kode ., kode file, or kode new.md for a file that does not exist yet (written when you save).
  • The default editor for text and code files in one click, from Settings ▸ Files.
  • Unsaved changes are asked about in a sheet, document by document, when you close or quit.
  • Changes on disk reload by themselves when nothing is unsaved, keeping the caret; otherwise Kode asks.
  • Each folder remembers: the files its window had open, its splits and size come back when you open the folder again; a folder opened for the first time starts empty, and Kode launched alone opens without a folder.
  • Safe saves through an atomic write, keeping the file's encoding, byte order mark and line endings.

Encodings

Kode detects a file's encoding when it opens it — a byte order mark, valid UTF-8, or Windows-1252 — and keeps it.

  • Reopen with or save with another of 20 encodings from the status bar: Unicode, Latin and Windows code pages, Mac Roman, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Cyrillic…
  • Line endings (LF / CRLF) shown and changed from the status bar.
  • Never loses a character: saving refuses characters the encoding lacks.

Appearance

A Mac app all the way through, from the shared Rask modules of the rask-project apps.

  • A see-through window, blurred, with its opacity in Settings; the code can sit on the window's color instead.
  • The system's colors: the text, the selection and the caret's block in your accent color, following light and dark live.
  • Light, dark or as the system, and the theme's matching version.
  • SF Symbols for every icon, and the Finder's icons in the sidebar.
  • Any font and size; ⌘+ ⌘- ⌘0 zoom.

Performance

Kode's editing surface is a C++ item drawing into Qt Quick's scene graph. Nothing per keystroke or per frame grows with the file.

  • A piece table holds the text with an index of its lines; typing grows one piece and never copies the text.
  • Only the lines in view are laid out and drawn, each a scene-graph node moved by its matrix as you scroll.
  • Highlighting and search run in slices of a few milliseconds ahead of the view; a jump far down a large file colors the lines in view at once, from a guess, until the slices get there.
  • Large files open in the background: from 2 MB on, reading and decoding happen off the main thread, and the window never waits.
Open 100 MB to the first frame83 ms (TextEdit: 19.6 s)
Worst keystroke at 100 MB12.9 ms (TextEdit: 1.2 s)
Color a jump to the end of 100 MB7 ms (from the top: 22 s)
Memory with 100 MB of C++ open500 MB (VS Code: 1.2 GB, Xcode: 3.2 GB)

Release build on an Apple M1; the method is in editor-bench.md.

Updates

Kode updates itself. Once a day it looks for a newer release, downloads it in the background and installs it when you quit; Kode ▸ Check for Updates… checks right away and shows what changed.

  • Signed updates: each one is checked against Kode's key before it installs, and opens without the quarantine step.
  • Settings ▸ Updates turns the automatic checks and downloads off, and picks Stable only or Stable and beta.
